## business — The Business Cycle
The US economy is in late-cycle stagflation masked by headline metrics. Growth
composition is deteriorating under resilient aggregates: manufacturing PMI in/near
contraction, industrial production falling, construction weak ex-data-center.
Consumer spending holds in headline but composition rots — rising credit
delinquencies (subprime auto at crisis levels), discretionary weakening while
non-discretionary holds. Labor is late-cycle: leading indicators (claims, openings,
quits, hiring) deteriorating while the lagging unemployment rate hasn't caught up
(6-12mo lag). Real wages turning negative is the load-bearing signal — it compresses
the discretionary + retirement-contribution capacity of the cohorts that drive both,
setting up the behavioral transition.

## credit — The Credit Cycle
Credit stress is building across categories, more advanced than disclosure suggests.
The $3.8T IG maturity wall (2026-2028) is structural refinancing risk with 54% of IG
rated BBB (concentrated downgrade risk). Private credit is a specific stress point —
BDC losses (BCRED's first monthly loss since 2022), software-loan concentration,
redemption queues signaling forced selling. The load-bearing signal is institutional
revealed-preference: banks buying CDS protection on BDC exposure — harder to fake
than commentary, indicating internal risk committees price losses public statements
don't acknowledge. Risk is concentrated in private markets + regional banks
(real-estate exposure), making mark-to-market discovery slow.

## liquidity — The Liquidity Cycle (incl. yen / FX plumbing)
The Fed has effectively ended QT and is running "non-QE QE" — T-bill purchases
expanding the balance sheet without announcement, while messaging gradual
normalization. Structural reserve scarcity shows in ops data: record SRF usage, EFFR
pinned at IOER. The binding constraint is dealer balance-sheet capacity — record
Treasury issuance meets un-expanded primary-dealer capacity, forcing Fed
intervention. Fed policy space is narrower than headlines suggest (QT-end + liquidity
injection already deployed; rate cuts face the inflation constraint). Includes the
FX/global-capital plumbing (yen carry, long-end defense) where the same
duration/liquidity stress transmits internationally.

## geopolitical — The Geopolitical Cycle
The Iran war (Feb 28 2026) produced sustained Strait of Hormuz disruption (~20% of
global oil, 25% of LNG) and remains acute regardless of ceasefire dynamics — a "dual
blockade," unresolved underlying tensions, IEA-called "largest supply disruption in
the history of the global oil market." The signal is physical supply loss across
shared-infrastructure commodities, not headline crude — oil production (-10M bpd
Gulf), LNG (Ras Laffan, -17% Qatari capacity, multi-year rebuilds), plus the cascade
through helium, LPG, aluminum, sulfur/fertilizer, petrochemicals. Elevated insurance
+ security costs and a persistent energy risk premium regardless of military
ceasefire.

## housing — The Housing Cycle
Housing is a distinct cycle (not an input to others) in a dysfunctional equilibrium:
record-low transaction volume with record-high median prices ($408,800), first-time
buyer share at record lows, affordability the worst in 25 years. The load-bearing
dynamic is the "golden handcuffs" — sub-4% 2020-21 mortgage holders disincentivized
to sell — producing structural undersupply; sub-7% rates haven't restored
transactions. Tentative stabilization (not collapse, not recovery). Feeds the other
cycles: mortgage delinquencies → credit, construction employment → business,
regional-bank RE exposure → credit linkage.

## convergence — The Convergence Node (cross-cycle, NOT a cycle)
Where the 5 cycles converge and transmit. Holds the synthesis (synth- branches: the
plumbing break, the H2 2026 convergence window, no-marginal-buyer, rotation-not-meltup)
and the masking/persistence mechanism (branch-durability: the ~$500B AI-capex
substitution masking real-economy weakness, power-grid the binding constraint; and the
$50B/mo 401k mechanical bid that breaks via a 4-stage behavioral cascade). This is
where cross-cycle transmission edges live — the examination of how one cycle's stress
propagates to another (oil→FX→equity-selling→yields→mortgage; the capex→credit
off-book financing; housing↔credit feedback).
